The match goes to a super over. The bowler is at the top of his run-up. And you get a two-wheeler ad. You paid for this plan in January, specifically so this would not happen.

That feeling has a name in the industry, and it is not a bug. It is the business model.

## TL;DR

Paying an OTT platform in 2026 buys you **fewer ads on recorded content**. It does not buy silence. Every major platform except Netflix has written an exception into the plan for live sports and live shows, and the exception is where the money is.

| Platform (India) | Top paid plan | Ads after you pay? | The carve-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Netflix | ₹649/mo Premium | No | None. Netflix has not launched an ad tier in India [[5]] |
| YouTube Premium | ₹149/mo | No | None on YouTube videos |
| JioHotstar | ₹299/mo Premium | Partly | "Ad-free entertainment, **except for live sports and other live shows**" [[1]] |
| Prime Video | Prime + ₹129/mo add-on | Partly | "free ad-supported programming, live TV, and sports events" [[3]] |

> The short version: you are not paying to remove ads. You are paying to remove *some* ads from *some* content, and the wording that tells you which is in the plan page, not the ad break.

## What changed, and when

**Prime Video India switched on 17 June 2025.** After nearly nine years of ad-free streaming in India, Amazon began running "limited advertisements" on movies and shows [[2]]. The Prime membership price did not change. Instead, an add-on appeared: ₹129 a month or ₹699 a year to go back to what you already had [[2]].

**The ad load is 4 to 6 minutes per hour of content** [[4]]. For a 50-minute episode that is roughly one TV-style break. Amazon's own framing was volume restraint, not absence: "We remain focused on prioritising ad innovation over volume and Prime Video will now include limited advertisements" [[4]].

**JioHotstar priced the exception openly.** In its 21 January 2026 announcement of monthly plans, JioStar described the ₹299 Premium tier as "Ad-free entertainment, except for live sports and other live shows" [[1]]. Mobile (₹79) and Super (₹149) are ad-supported outright.

**And the add-on has its own exception.** Even Prime Video's paid ad-free upgrade says plainly: "Some content will continue to include advertising, such as free ad-supported programming, live TV, and sports events" [[3]]. So on a live match you can pay twice and still watch ads.

## The money math, in one number

Here is the part that made me stop being angry about it.

Viacom18 paid **₹23,758 crore for IPL digital rights for the 2023 to 2027 seasons** [[6]]. That is about ₹4,752 crore for a single season, for one tournament, before a single camera, server, or engineer is paid for.

Now price that against subscriptions. At ₹2,199 a year for JioHotstar Premium, covering that one rights fee alone needs roughly **2.16 crore annual Premium subscribers** paying for a full year and spending nothing on anything else. India had 216 million paid video subscriptions in total across every platform in 2025 [[7]], split across hundreds of plans starting at ₹79.

The subscription pool in India is real but small. FICCI and EY put digital subscription revenue at **₹163 billion in 2025, up 60%** [[7]]. Digital advertising in the same report came in at **₹947 billion, up 26%** [[7]]. That advertising figure covers all of digital, not streaming alone, so it is not an apples-to-apples comparison. It still tells you which side of the business has the deeper pocket, and which side a ₹299 plan cannot outbid.

Live sport is sold to advertisers by the minute against a guaranteed audience. Your subscription is sold to you by the month against a library. When a rights holder pays ₹4,752 crore a season, the ad minute during the super over is not the greedy extra. It is the main revenue line, and your subscription is the co-payment.

## So is it unjust?

I think the honest answer splits in two.

**Legally, in the biggest test case so far, no.** Prime Video subscribers in the US ran a consolidated class action arguing exactly the point you are making: I paid for ad-free, you took it away, that is a bait and switch. On 16 July 2025, Judge Barbara Jacobs Rothstein dismissed it with prejudice. The court's reasoning, quoted in the coverage of the order, was that adding ads "was not a price increase; it was a benefit modification, and such modification was specifically contemplated and authorized by the parties' governing agreements" [[8]]. The terms you accepted let them change the bundle. I could not find an Indian ruling on the same question, and Indian platforms use similar reservation-of-rights language.

**Commercially, the grievance is still fair, and it is about disclosure, not ads.** Nobody objects to a shop raising prices. What stings is paying for a defined thing and having the definition edited afterwards, quietly, on a page you last read in 2019. The fix a platform could make tomorrow costs nothing: print "ads during live sports" on the buy button, at the same size as the price. JioHotstar does say it in the plan copy. Very few people read plan copy while a match is starting.

So: not a legal injustice. A disclosure failure, and a real one.

## When paying is still worth it, and when it is not

**Worth it, if you watch series and films.** On recorded content the ad-free tiers do what they say. Removing 4 to 6 minutes an hour across a season of twenty episodes is nearly two hours of your life back.

**Worth it, if you want 4K and multiple screens.** On most Indian plans the top tier bundles resolution, simultaneous devices, and downloads. Ads are one line item of several, and the others are not carved out.

**Not worth it, if you subscribed only for the cricket.** This is the honest one. If your entire reason for the Premium tier is live sport, the ads are contractually guaranteed to stay, and you are paying mostly for resolution and device count. Downgrade a tier and check whether the picture actually bothers you.

**Not worth it, if you are stacking three platforms out of habit.** Three ₹299 plans is ₹10,764 a year. Rotate instead: one platform a month, cancelled when the show ends. Monthly plans exist now precisely because platforms know you will not do this. Prove them wrong.

## What you can actually do

**Read the carve-out before you pay, not after.** Search the plan page for the words "live" and "except". That is where the exception always lives.

**Pick the genuinely ad-free option if that is the whole point.** As of July 2026, Netflix has kept every India tier ad-free from ₹149 [[5]], and YouTube Premium removes ads on YouTube. If ad-free is your only requirement, buy the plan that promises it without an exception clause.

**Escalate on facts, not feelings.** If a platform's own plan page promises ad-free and you are served ads on that same content type, that is a specific, quotable discrepancy. A screenshot of the plan page next to a screenshot of the ad is a much stronger complaint than "I paid and still get ads".

## FAQ

**Why am I still seeing ads on Prime Video after paying for Prime?**
Prime membership by itself has included ads on movies and shows in India since 17 June 2025 [[2]]. Ad-free is a separate paid add-on at ₹129 a month or ₹699 a year [[2]], and even that add-on excludes live TV, sports events, and free ad-supported programming [[3]].

**Is JioHotstar Premium completely ad-free?**
No. JioStar's own plan description is "ad-free entertainment, except for live sports and other live shows" [[1]]. The ₹299 Premium tier removes ads from the library, not from a live match.

**Which OTT platform in India has no ads at all in 2026?**
Netflix, on every India tier from ₹149 to ₹649, has not launched an ad-supported plan in India [[5]]. YouTube Premium at ₹149 removes ads on YouTube itself.

**How many ads does Prime Video show per hour in India?**
Between 4 and 6 minutes of ads per hour of content [[4]].

**Can I get a refund if my paid plan started showing ads?**
The one large legal challenge to this failed. A US court held the change was a "benefit modification" allowed by the terms of service, and dismissed the class action with prejudice on 16 July 2025 [[8]]. A refund claim is stronger when the platform's current plan page contradicts what you were served, weaker when the change was announced and the terms reserve the right.

**Is it legal to show ads to paying subscribers?**
On the record so far, yes, where the subscriber agreement reserves the right to modify benefits [[8]]. That is a statement about contract law, not about whether the disclosure was good enough.

## My take

Streaming sold us a story: pay a little, and the ads go away forever. It was never a promise about ads. It was a promise about *this year's* ads, at *this year's* content cost, before anyone paid ₹23,758 crore for five seasons of one tournament.

The model that is actually arriving is the old one with a login. Cable charged you and ran ads too. The difference is cable never claimed otherwise.

You are allowed to be annoyed. Just aim it at the right thing: not the existence of ads, but the fact that the exception is printed in the terms and never on the button you clicked.
